# The catalogue

Two files, two questions the export poses and cannot answer:
`category_values.json` says what a category record's value *name* means, and
`quantity_types.json` says how a quantity record's *numbers* combine over a
day. They are separate files so that a documentation reshuffle among the
symptoms does not land in the same diff as one among the nutrients, and
`--only` refreshes either alone.

## What a category value means

A category record states its value as a name:

```xml
<Record type="HKCategoryTypeIdentifierLowerBackPain" value="HKCategoryValueSeverityModerate" …/>
```

The export's DTD declares that attribute as `value CDATA #IMPLIED` and can do
no better, because which names are legal depends on the record's type: a
severity for a symptom, a stage for sleep, `HKCategoryValueNotApplicable` for a
mindful session. The export never says which. Apple's HealthKit documentation
does, per identifier, and `category_values.json` is that mapping, generated by
[`tools/refresh_catalog.py`](../../tools/refresh_catalog.py).

## How a quantity's day adds up

A quantity record states a number, and the number alone does not say what a
day of them is:

```xml
<Record type="HKQuantityTypeIdentifierStepCount" unit="count" value="1200" …/>
<Record type="HKQuantityTypeIdentifierHeartRate" unit="count/min" value="62" …/>
```

Two step samples of 1200 and 500 are a day of 1700 steps. Two heart rates of
62 and 70 are a day of 66 bpm, and their sum is nothing. HealthKit calls this a
type's *aggregation style*, cumulative or discrete, and states it per
identifier in the discussion on the identifier's page:

> These samples use count units (described in `HKUnit`) and measure
> **cumulative** values (described in `HKQuantityAggregationStyle`).

`quantity_types.json` is those two words per type, plus Apple's grouping.
`daily_metrics` joins it and fills in `aggregation` and `day_value` — the sum
for cumulative types, the mean for discrete ones — while `total`, `mean`,
`min_value` and `max_value` all stay exactly as they were. The new column is a
signpost, not a replacement.

| Field | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- |
| `types.<id>.aggregation` | the adjective in that sentence: `cumulative` or `discrete`, or null where the page says neither |
| `types.<id>.family` | the index section: Activity, Nutrition, Vital signs, … |

The sentence is prose, which makes it a softer fact than the category link, so
the generator anchors on the `HKQuantityAggregationStyle` link rather than on
the words around it — three audio-exposure pages link a member of the
enumeration instead of the enumeration itself, which is the same shape the
category pages have. Three identifiers state nothing at all
(`WorkoutEffortScore` and friends have no discussion); they get null and no
`day_value`, which is what a type Apple adds tomorrow will get too.

**What `day_value` still cannot tell you** is which sources contributed. The
grouping is per type, day and unit — so where an iPhone and a Watch both
record steps, a cumulative `day_value` adds both sets up and reads higher than
the Health app does. This is not new to `day_value`; `total` always did it.
Three tables answer it, none of them here: `metric_summary` and
`daily_metrics_by_source` split by source, and `daily_metrics.deduplicated_value`
reconstructs the single de-duplicated number the app shows. That last one is a
heuristic — a per-day source priority the export cannot state — and lives in
`dedup.py` for the same reason the catalogue lives here: it is interpretation
laid beside the raw sum, never replacing it.

## What this is not

**It is not a second schema.** `applehealth/schema/` stays the source of truth
for what an export contains — elements, attributes, nesting. The catalogue only
attaches meaning to values an export already carries, and it is deliberately
powerless:

* Nothing on the import path reads it. Imports do not touch it at all; it is
  loaded by `derive`, into three tables the views and `daily_metrics` join
  against.
* `records.value_text` keeps the raw name, and `total`, `mean`, `min_value`
  and `max_value` keep their numbers. What the catalogue adds is added beside
  them, never in place of them.
* Every join is an outer one. A type or value the catalogue has never heard of
  — and Apple adds both with every iOS release — comes through with its raw
  name and its aggregates intact; only the interpretation is missing.

So the catalogue going stale costs you a column, not a record. That is the
opposite trade from the DTD, where being wrong means data lands in `unhandled`
and gets reported.

## Where the data comes from

The documentation pages are rendered client-side and yield nothing to a plain
fetch, but the JSON behind them is served under a parallel URL:

| Page | JSON |
| --- | --- |
| `developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkcategorytypeidentifier` | `…/tutorials/data/documentation/healthkit/hkcategorytypeidentifier.json` |
| `…/documentation/healthkit/symptom-type-identifiers` | `…/tutorials/data/documentation/healthkit/symptom-type-identifiers.json` |
| `…/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier` | `…/tutorials/data/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier.json` |

Four fields carry the load:

* **`metadata.externalID`** — `c:@HKCategoryTypeIdentifierBloating`. This is the
  Objective-C name, which is exactly the spelling the XML uses. The Swift name
  on the same page is `bloating`; deriving the export spelling from it would be
  a guess, and this is the fact.
* **the `HKCategoryValue…` link in a type's discussion** — how the docs say
  which enumeration a type's values come from. Some pages link the enumeration
  (`HKCategoryValueSeverity`), others one of its members
  (`HKCategoryValue.notApplicable`); both name it.
* **the `HKQuantityAggregationStyle` link in a quantity type's discussion** —
  the sentence around it holds the word `cumulative` or `discrete`. Here too
  some pages link the enumeration and some a member
  (`discreteEquivalentContinuousLevel`).
* **`topicSections`** — Apple's own grouping, which becomes `family` and
  `topic`.

## Fields, and which of them are Apple's

Everything in the generated files is read from the documentation, with one
exception, marked below. The category file:

| Field | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- |
| `types.<id>.enum` | the value-enumeration link on the type's page |
| `types.<id>.family` | the index section: Symptoms, Reproductive Health, … |
| `types.<id>.topic` | the finer section on the symptom page: Musculoskeletal, … |
| `enums.<e>.members[].name` | enumeration name + case name, per the ObjC symbol |
| `enums.<e>.members[].short` | the same, minus the enumeration prefix |
| `enums.<e>.scale` | **a judgement — see below** |
| `enums.<e>.members[].ordinal` | position on that ladder, for scales only |

**`scale` is the one judgement call.** Apple lists an enumeration's members in
an order but never says the order means anything, so the generator carries a
short explicit list of the enumerations whose members really are a ladder
(`SCALE_ENUMS`) rather than inferring it. The order is not always one:
`HKCategoryValueVaginalBleeding` reads Unspecified, Light, Medium, Heavy,
*None* — inferring from that would rank `None` above `Heavy`. Members that sit
beside a ladder rather than on it (`Unspecified`) get no ordinal even in a
scale.

An `ordinal` is for sorting and plotting. It is not a measurement: the distance
from Mild to Moderate is not a quantity, and averaging a column of them means
nothing.

The quantity file is the two fields listed further up, and neither is a
judgement: Apple names the aggregation style in words, and the grouping is the
index heading.

## Refreshing them

```bash
poe refresh-catalog
```

Some two hundred and thirty requests, a few minutes, and the result is
committed. Review the diff as documentation moving rather than code changing.
`--dry-run` prints the counts without writing, and `--only categories` /
`--only quantities` refreshes one file so the other's diff stays out of the
review.

The generator fails loudly if the pages stop looking the way it expects, rather
than writing a half-filled catalogue — a missing entry would otherwise show up
months later as a column of raw `HKCategoryValue…` names, or as a `day_value`
that quietly went empty everywhere.

## What is deliberately not here

* **Apple's prose.** The descriptions on those pages are theirs; identifier
  names, enumeration membership and grouping are facts, and only those are
  copied.
* **Units.** The same sentence that names a quantity type's aggregation style
  names its unit family — "count units", "energy units". The export states the
  actual unit on every record, so copying Apple's word for it would add a
  second, vaguer answer to a question already answered.
* **Types Apple documents nothing for.** One category type at the time of
  writing (`HKCategoryTypeIdentifierHypertensionEvent`, with no value
  enumeration) and three quantity types with no discussion at all
  (`WorkoutEffortScore`, `EstimatedWorkoutEffortScore`,
  `AppleSleepingBreathingDisturbances`). They get no translation and no
  `day_value`, which is the same thing that happens to anything new.
